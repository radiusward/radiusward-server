# ADR 0038: RADIUS over DTLS

Status: Accepted (2026-11-10). Phase 5, work package 5.14 ([plans/phase-5-auth.md](../plans/phase-5-auth.md)).

## Context
RadSec (ADR 0034) protects RADIUS with TLS over TCP. Some NAS and roaming federations use DTLS instead (RFC 7360):
the same protection over UDP, port 2083, with no TCP connection to keep up and no head-of-line blocking between
packets. A NAS that already sends RADIUS over UDP only has to wrap each datagram.

The JDK has DTLS 1.2 in `SSLEngine` (`SSLContext.getInstance("DTLS")`); it has no DTLS 1.3. Like TLS for RadSec,
DTLS is a transport in front of the gateway's existing decision paths.

## Decision
- **Same agent as RadSec**: `RadsecAgent` also reads a UDP socket (`radsec.dtls.enabled`, `radsec.dtls.port`, 2083)
  on its selector, with or without the TCP listener (`radsec.enabled`).
  - It stays the only producer on the rings to the `AuthAgent` and `UdpAgent` (`RadsecLink`), so nothing changes
    on their side.
  - Associations take the route slots after the TCP connections. Replies, stale replies after a close, and CoA
    requests find them the same way as connections.
  - Certificate, key, client CAs, revocation and reload are the RadSec ones (`radsec.certificate`, `radsec.key`,
    `radsec.ca`, ...). `TlsCredentials.currentDtls()` builds a DTLS context from the same files, and builds it again
    after a reload.
- **Associations**: one per NAS address and port, each with its `SSLEngine`, client certificate required, DTLS 1.2
  only.
  - Only a ClientHello starts one, and only from an address whose NAS entry allows DTLS (or from any address while
    entries are identified by certificate alone). Other datagrams from unknown peers are discarded before any state
    is kept.
  - At most `radsec.dtls.associations` (256); a ClientHello beyond that is dropped.
  - The JDK's server sends a HelloVerifyRequest with a cookie before the expensive part of the handshake.
  - A ClientHello in epoch 0 from the address of an established association replaces it: the NAS restarted
    (RFC 6347 §4.2.8).
  - Closed by close_notify, by `radsec.dtls.idle.timeout` (15 min without traffic, so a NAS that vanished does not
    hold its slot), or by `radsec.handshake.timeout` (10 s) if the handshake does not finish.
- **Which NAS**: after the handshake as for RadSec, with `nas.<name>.dtls`, `nas.<name>.dtls.identity` and
  `nas.<name>.dtls.secret`.
  - The secret defaults to `radius/dtls` (RFC 7360 §2.1).
  - An entry without `address` but with `dtls = true` and `dtls.identity` is identified by its certificate alone.
  - The link holds the DTLS entries after the RadSec ones, with their own secret and the entry's id. An event has the
    same keys over UDP, RadSec and DTLS, and the cluster deduplicates it across all three.
- **Packets**: one RADIUS packet per DTLS record (RFC 7360 §3).
  - A record whose RADIUS length is below 20 or beyond the record is discarded and the association kept, as a bad
    datagram is discarded over UDP. RadSec instead closes the connection, because its framing is lost.
  - The JDK's maximum record size (about 16 KB) is kept, so every RADIUS packet fits in one record. A smaller limit
    would split packets across records. Datagrams above the path MTU, such as a handshake flight with a long
    certificate chain, rely on IP fragmentation, as large RADIUS packets over UDP do. Keep certificate chains short.
- **Retransmission** (RFC 6347 §4.2.4): a handshake flight without an answer is sent again after
  `radsec.dtls.retransmit` (1 s), doubling up to 8 s. Retransmitted requests after the handshake are the NAS's
  business, as over UDP; the response caches answer them.
- **CoA and Disconnect** (ADR 0036): a NAS with an established RadSec connection gets them there. Otherwise, with a
  DTLS association, it gets them over that association (RFC 7360 §2.4), signed with its DTLS secret. Otherwise they
  go over UDP to port 3799.
- **Metrics**: `radiusward_gateway_dtls_total{result}`, with `retransmissions`, `not_associated` and `restarted`
  besides the RadSec results, and `radiusward_gateway_dtls_associations`.
- **Not covered**:
  - DTLS 1.3, because the JDK has none.
  - The gateway as a DTLS client to home servers (ADR 0037 proxies over UDP and RadSec). NAPTR records for
    `aaa+auth:radius.dtls.udp` are skipped by discovery.
  - Session resumption across associations is left to the JDK, with the RadSec session cache and
    `radsec.session.timeout`; it is counted (`handshakes_resumed`) but not tested.

## Consequences
- A NAS that speaks RADIUS over UDP can be protected end to end without a TCP connection per NAS, and through the
  same NAS entries, decisions, commit boundary and deduplication as every other transport.
- Each datagram on the DTLS path allocates its source address (the JDK's `DatagramChannel.receive`) and passes
  through the engine. This is no worse than RadSec, and the plain UDP hot path is unchanged (`HotPathAllocationIT`).
- An address can hold at most one association and the table is bounded, so a flood of ClientHellos from spoofed
  addresses of allowed NAS costs at most `radsec.dtls.associations` engines until the handshake timeout. The cookie
  exchange keeps the costly part of the handshake for peers that can receive at their address.

## Tests
- `DtlsIT`, against a real gateway with the JDK's DTLS as the NAS:
  - Access-Requests and Status-Server, 50 in flight on one association.
  - Lost handshake flights retransmitted by the gateway's timer while the NAS stays silent.
  - Missing and foreign client certificates; a certificate that does not name the entry.
  - An entry identified by its certificate alone.
  - A restarted NAS on the same port replacing its association.
  - Datagrams outside DTLS and malformed packets discarded.
  - Idle and unfinished associations timed out.
  - RadSec and DTLS on one gateway.
- `RadsecAccountingIT`, with 3 cluster members: a Start over DTLS, the Interim over RadSec and the Start again over
  UDP make one session and one duplicate.
- `CoaIT`: a Disconnect-Request over the NAS's DTLS association, signed with `radius/dtls`.
- `RadsecConfigTest`: defaults, DTLS alone or beside TCP, configuration errors.
- `RadsecproxyIT`: a second radsecproxy 1.11 instance (`testing/radsec-client`) forwards over DTLS 1.2 what
  `radclient` (PAP accept and reject) and `eapol_test` (PEAP with crypto-binding, TTLS) send it over UDP. The MS-MPPE
  keys are encrypted with `radius/dtls`, re-encrypted by radsecproxy, and must still match the peer's MSK.
